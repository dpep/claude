# San Francisco Neighborhoods

Reference for the `realtor` persona, as of late September 2026. Prices here come from a one-year MLS window and date fastest; character, housing stock, microclimate and hazards change slowly. Sourced facts carry inline links. Characterizations of vibe, housing stock and trade-offs that are not linked are general, widely held local knowledge — treat them as starting points to verify on the ground, not as facts to quote.

## How to read this

- SF MLS (San Francisco Association of Realtors) divides the city into 10 districts and ~89 sub-districts ([SocketSite](https://socketsite.com/archives/2009/06/san_francisco_real_estate_districts_maps_and_neighborho.html), [Jennifer Rosdail district map](https://www.jenniferrosdail.com/san-francisco-neighborhood-map/)). MLS districts are not the same as Supervisor districts — don't mix them.
- Price tiers below use MLS-derived medians for July 15 2025 – July 16 2026 compiled by a local realtor team ([Team Kho, 2026 district survey](https://team-kho.com/san-francisco-housing-market-update-2026/)). Citywide: single-family median $1.85M (2,156 sales), condo median $1.185M (2,291 sales). District medians blend very different sub-neighborhoods; small-sample districts (D6, D8 houses) are noisy.

| District | House median | Condo median |

Notable sub-district medians from the same source: Noe Valley $2.88M (110 sales), Eureka Valley/Dolores Heights $3.48M, Pacific Heights $6.85M, Cow Hollow $7.89M (20 sales).

Microclimate in one line: the west side (Outer Richmond, Outer Sunset, near Ocean Beach) is the fog belt, routinely 55–62°F in summer; the Mission, Castro, Potrero Hill, Bernal Heights and SoMa/Mission Bay are the sun belt, often mid-70s; Noe Valley, Haight/Cole Valley and downtown are transitional ([SFBayWeather](https://www.sfbayweather.com/learn/san-francisco-weather-by-neighborhood), [SFBayWeather microclimates](https://www.sfbayweather.com/learn/san-francisco-microclimates)). Summer is the foggiest season on the west side; September–October is the warm season citywide.

Rail spine (for commuters):
- BART in SF: Embarcadero, Montgomery, Powell, Civic Center (Market St), 16th St Mission, 24th St Mission, Glen Park, Balboa Park — the Mission/Glen Park/Balboa Park corridor is the BART-rich residential band.
- Muni Metro: J, K, L, M, N light rail plus T Third; the Central Subway extended the T to Chinatown with full service from January 7, 2023 ([SFMTA](https://www.sfmta.com/blog/new-t-third-route-central-subway-starting-january-7)).
- Caltrain: 4th & King, 22nd St, Bayshore; fully electrified service since September 21, 2024 ([Caltrain](https://www.caltrain.com/news/caltrain-commences-fully-electrified-service)).
- Freeways: I-280 and US-101 exit the city on the south/southeast side, so D9, D10 and southern D4/D5 (Glen Park, Bernal, Potrero, Excelsior, Portola) are the easiest Peninsula drives; the Marina/Richmond/Sunset are the longest.

## District-by-district

### District 1 — Northwest
Neighborhoods: Sea Cliff, Lake Street, Inner/Central/Outer Richmond, Jordan Park/Laurel Heights, Lone Mountain ([Rosdail](https://www.jenniferrosdail.com/san-francisco-neighborhood-map/)).
- Character: residential, family-oriented, strong Asian (esp. Chinese) food and retail scene on Clement St and Geary; Sea Cliff and Lake Street are the quiet high end.
- Housing: 1900s–1920s Edwardians and flats in the Inner Richmond; 1920s–40s row houses and "Richmond specials" (1960s–70s stucco apartments) further out; large detached homes in Sea Cliff.
- Price: upper-middle; $2.49M house median; condos/TIC units in two-to-six-unit Edwardian buildings are common.
- Microclimate: foggy and cool, worst toward the Outer Richmond; Inner Richmond milder.
- Transit: bus-only (38/38R Geary, 1 California, 5/5R Fulton); no rail. Long drive to the Peninsula.
- Parks: Golden Gate Park, Presidio, Lands End, Mountain Lake Park.
- Family: high. Private-school share is high in wealthy pockets — Sea Cliff is over 75% ([SF Chronicle, Oct 2025, via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/map-shows-f-neighborhoods-send-110019204.html)).
- Trade-offs: fog; no rail; Geary/California/Clement are upzoned to allow roughly 6–8 story buildings (65 ft called out for California and Clement) under the Family Zoning Plan ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2025/12/02/family-zoning-supervisors-vote-daniel-lurie/), [SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/sf-family-zoning-plan)); earthquake-induced landslide zones along the Outer Richmond and Sea Cliff bluffs ([summary of DataSF seismic hazard layer](https://data.sf.gov/-/San-Francisco-Seismic-Hazard-Zones/7ahv-68ap)).

### District 2 — West (Sunset & Parkside)
Neighborhoods: Inner/Central/Outer Sunset, Inner/Outer Parkside, Golden Gate Heights.
- Character: quiet, residential, family, strong Chinese-American community; Inner Sunset (Irving/9th Ave near UCSF) is the livelier pocket; the Outer Sunset has a surf/beach-town vibe.
- Housing: 1920s–40s stucco tract homes ("Doelger" style — living space over garage) on standard 25x100 lots; Golden Gate Heights has larger view homes on steep streets.
- Price: middle; $1.80M house median; per a local survey D2 "prices lowest against value and closes fastest" and sells over list most of the time ([Team Kho](https://team-kho.com/san-francisco-housing-market-update-2026/)).
- Microclimate: foggiest in the city in summer, especially west of ~19th Ave.
- Transit: N Judah and L Taraval light rail (L restored after multi-year street work); 19th Ave (CA-1) connects south to 280.
- Parks: Golden Gate Park, Ocean Beach, Stern Grove, Sunset Dunes (former Upper Great Highway).
- Family: very high; home to several of the most competitive public elementary programs (see schools section).
- Trade-offs: fog; Irving, Judah, Taraval, Noriega corridors upzoned (plan keeps ~40 ft caps in the more residential transit-adjacent areas) ([SF Standard, SB 79 context](https://sfstandard.com/2025/12/02/family-zoning-supervisors-vote-daniel-lurie/), [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/san-francisco/2026/03/04/lawsuits-could-trigger-blunter-upzoning-in-san-francisco/)). Great Highway: closed to cars in March 2025 under Prop K and reopened as Sunset Dunes park in April 2025; Prop G on the November 2026 ballot would reopen it to cars on weekdays ([SF Rec & Park timeline](https://sfrecpark.org/1651/Sunset-Dunes-Background-Timeline), [GrowSF Prop G](https://growsf.org/voter-guide/san-francisco-voter-guide-november-2026-election/measures/prop-g/)) — traffic on Sunset Blvd/Lower Great Highway depends on that outcome. Golden Gate Heights has landslide-zone exposure ([DataSF seismic hazard zones](https://data.sf.gov/-/San-Francisco-Seismic-Hazard-Zones/7ahv-68ap)).

### District 3 — Southwest
Neighborhoods: Lake Shore, Lakeside, Merced Manor, Merced Heights, Ingleside, Ingleside Heights, Oceanview.
- Character: suburban-feeling, near SF State, Stonestown and Lake Merced; Lakeside/Merced Manor are quiet detached-house enclaves, Ingleside/Oceanview are more working-class and diverse.
- Housing: 1930s–50s detached and semi-detached homes; Parkmerced towers and garden apartments; few Victorians.
- Price: lowest-to-middle; $1.40M house median, $692K condo median (22 sales).
- Microclimate: cool and foggy near Lake Merced, somewhat milder inland.
- Transit: M Ocean View; Balboa Park BART is close to the eastern edge; 280 and 19th Ave nearby — good for Peninsula commuters.
- Parks: Lake Merced, Harding Park, Stern Grove nearby.
- Family: high; large lots by SF standards.
- Trade-offs: fewer walkable commercial streets; 19th Ave is a noisy, dangerous arterial; Lake Shore has bluff landslide exposure.

### District 4 — Central Southwest
Neighborhoods: St. Francis Wood, Forest Hill, West Portal, Forest Knolls, Diamond Heights, Midtown Terrace, Miraloma Park, Sunnyside, Balboa Terrace, Ingleside Terrace, Mt. Davidson Manor, Sherwood Forest, Monterey Heights, Westwood Highlands.
- Character: "the suburbs inside the city" — the residence parks (St. Francis Wood, Forest Hill, Sea Cliff-like planned enclaves), West Portal's village main street.
- Housing: 1910s–40s detached homes, some large and architect-designed in the residence parks; 1950s–60s Diamond Heights/Midtown Terrace houses and condos.
- Price: upper-middle houses ($2.25M median) but cheap condos ($755K median, mostly Diamond Heights); St. Francis Wood is high end.
- Microclimate: mixed; the west slope of Twin Peaks catches fog and wind, Sunnyside/Miraloma are sunnier.
- Transit: K, L, M converge at West Portal and run through the Twin Peaks tunnel to downtown; Glen Park and Balboa Park BART at the edges; 280 access is good.
- Parks: Mt. Davidson, Glen Canyon, Stern Grove.
- Family: very high. West Portal Elementary's TK program was the single most competitive in the district in 2025 (277 waitlisted for 18 seats) ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2025/03/26/sf-public-schools-ranking-admissions/)).
- Trade-offs: steep streets; landslide zones on Twin Peaks, Midtown Terrace, Forest Hill, Diamond Heights ([DataSF seismic hazard zones](https://data.sf.gov/-/San-Francisco-Seismic-Hazard-Zones/7ahv-68ap)); car-dependent away from West Portal.

### District 5 — Central
Neighborhoods: Glen Park, Haight Ashbury, Noe Valley, Twin Peaks, Cole Valley/Parnassus Heights, Buena Vista/Ashbury Heights, Corona Heights, Clarendon Heights, Duboce Triangle, Eureka Valley/Dolores Heights (Castro), Mission Dolores ([search summary of MLS sub-districts 5A–5M](https://www.jenniferrosdail.com/san-francisco-neighborhood-map/)).
- Character: the city's most in-demand family belt. Noe Valley (24th St, strollers), the Castro, Cole Valley, Glen Park's village; Haight is livelier and grittier on Haight St.
- Housing: Victorians and Edwardians dominate, often split into 2–3 unit TICs/condos; some 1920s–40s houses and modern rebuilds on the hills.
- Price: high; $2.80M house median, $1.50M condo median; Noe Valley $2.88M, Eureka Valley/Dolores Heights $3.48M ([Team Kho](https://team-kho.com/san-francisco-housing-market-update-2026/)). Condos here "behave like small houses" — 72% sold over list.
- Microclimate: Castro/Dolores/Mission Dolores sunny; Noe Valley transitional (warmer than the Sunset, slightly cooler than the Mission); Cole Valley a sheltered pocket; Twin Peaks windy and foggy ([SFBayWeather](https://www.sfbayweather.com/learn/san-francisco-weather-by-neighborhood)).
- Transit: J Church, K/L/M via Castro station, N Judah through Cole Valley; Glen Park BART; 280 from Glen Park is excellent for the Peninsula.
- Parks: Dolores Park, Buena Vista, Corona Heights, Glen Canyon, Duboce Park, Golden Gate Park Panhandle.
- Family: very high; Clarendon and Alvarado are among the most requested schools in the district ([SF Chronicle via search summary](https://www.sfchronicle.com/sf/article/popular-sfusd-schools-assignment-21305291.php), [sfeducation substack](https://sfeducation.substack.com/p/what-new-public-data-tells-us-about)).
- Trade-offs: hills, very tight parking, old housing (knob-and-tube, foundations). Landslide zones on Twin Peaks, Clarendon Heights, the Castro, Dolores Heights and Noe Valley slopes ([DataSF seismic hazard zones](https://data.sf.gov/-/San-Francisco-Seismic-Hazard-Zones/7ahv-68ap)).

### District 6 — Central North
Neighborhoods: Hayes Valley, North of Panhandle (NOPA), Alamo Square, Western Addition, Anza Vista, Lower Pacific Heights.
- Character: Hayes Valley is boutique retail and restaurants; NOPA/Alamo Square are Victorian showpieces with Divisadero's restaurant row; Western Addition mixes Victorians with mid-century public and subsidized housing.
- Housing: Victorians (many converted to condos/TICs), post-2010 mid-rise condos around Market/Octavia.
- Price: condo-driven — $1.25M condo median (230 sales); only 27 house sales ($3.44M median), so the house figure is thin.
- Microclimate: transitional; windy near Market/Octavia.
- Transit: central — Muni Metro at Van Ness/Church, BART Civic Center within reach, many bus lines (5, 21, 22, 24, 38, 49 Van Ness BRT); Central Freeway to 101.
- Parks: Alamo Square, the Panhandle, Patricia's Green, Jefferson Square.
- Family: moderate-to-high; Alamo Elementary is among the most requested ([sfeducation substack](https://sfeducation.substack.com/p/what-new-public-data-tells-us-about)).
- Trade-offs: street conditions vary block by block (especially nearer Civic Center and the Tenderloin edge); car break-ins; Central Freeway noise/air near Octavia.

### District 7 — North
Neighborhoods: Pacific Heights, Presidio Heights, Cow Hollow, Marina.
- Character: the city's wealthiest residential district; Fillmore St and Union St shopping; the Marina skews young professional on Chestnut St.
- Housing: grand Victorians, Edwardians and mansions in Pacific/Presidio Heights; 1920s–30s Mediterranean-style "Marina-style" homes and flats in the Marina; many condos in converted buildings.
- Price: top tier — $6.35M house median; Pacific Heights $6.85M, Cow Hollow $7.89M; condos $1.64M.
- Microclimate: mixed; the Marina gets bay wind and fog off the Golden Gate.
- Transit: bus only (22, 30, 45, 1, 3, 24); no rail. Farthest from the Peninsula freeways.
- Parks: Presidio, Crissy Field, Marina Green, Lafayette Park, Alta Plaza.
- Family: high, but private school dominant — the Marina and Sea Cliff both send over 75% of children to private school ([SF Chronicle via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/map-shows-f-neighborhoods-send-110019204.html)).
- Trade-offs: the Marina is built on fill and liquefied in both 1906 and 1989 (Loma Prieta building collapses at Divisadero/Beach) ([search summary citing SFGate/CGS](https://earthquakesafety.com/resource-seismic-maps)); Lombard St and Van Ness are upzoned for taller buildings ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2025/12/02/family-zoning-supervisors-vote-daniel-lurie/)); Lombard (US-101) traffic and noise.

### District 8 — Northeast
Neighborhoods: Russian Hill, Nob Hill, Telegraph Hill, North Beach, Financial District, North Waterfront, Downtown, Van Ness/Civic Center, Tenderloin.
- Character: dense and urban — North Beach cafes, Russian/Nob Hill views, downtown high-rises, the Tenderloin's severe street conditions.
- Housing: condos and co-ops (vintage mid-rises on the hills, modern high-rises downtown and along the waterfront); few houses (30 sales, $4.24M median).
- Price: condo market — $1.01M median (436 sales); wide spread from Tenderloin studios to Russian Hill view penthouses.
- Microclimate: moderate; windy and foggy on hilltops.
- Transit: the best in the city — BART and Muni Metro on Market, the Central Subway to Chinatown, cable cars; Bay Bridge access.
- Walkability: Chinatown and the Tenderloin both score 100 on Walk Score; Nob Hill 99 ([Walk Score](https://www.walkscore.com/CA/San_Francisco)).
- Family: lower; Chinatown and the Tenderloin have the lowest private-school rates (under 10%) ([SF Chronicle via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/map-shows-f-neighborhoods-send-110019204.html)).
- Trade-offs: HOA dues and older co-op financing quirks; steep hills; Telegraph Hill has earthquake-induced landslide exposure ([The Front Steps](https://thefrontsteps.com/2010/11/22/san-francisco-neighborhoods-prone-to-liquefaction-and-earthquake-induced-landslides-bedrock-vs-landfill-take-two/)); Fisherman's Wharf/North Waterfront and downtown east of Montgomery are on fill (liquefaction) ([search summary citing CGS](https://earthquakesafety.com/resource-seismic-maps)); Tenderloin/Civic Center street conditions.

### District 9 — East
Neighborhoods: Bernal Heights (9A), Inner Mission (9C), Mission Bay (9D), Potrero Hill (9E), SoMa (9F), Yerba Buena (9G), South Beach (9H), Dogpatch (9J).
- Character: the sun belt. The Mission's Latino heritage and food scene; Bernal's hilltop village feel on Cortland; Potrero Hill views; Dogpatch/Mission Bay newer and denser, near UCSF Mission Bay and Chase Center.
- Housing: Victorians/Edwardians and 1900s cottages in the Mission, Bernal and Potrero; lofts and post-2000 condo towers in SoMa, South Beach, Mission Bay, Dogpatch.
- Price: $1.80M house median; the condo volume leader — $1.02M median, 823 sales ([Team Kho](https://team-kho.com/san-francisco-housing-market-update-2026/)).
- Microclimate: warmest and sunniest part of the city — Mission, Potrero, Bernal ([SFBayWeather](https://www.sfbayweather.com/learn/san-francisco-weather-by-neighborhood)).
- Transit: 16th and 24th St BART; T Third along Mission Bay/Dogpatch; Caltrain at 4th & King and 22nd St; 101 and 280 right there — the best Peninsula commute in the city.
- Parks: Dolores Park (edge), Bernal Hill, McLaren Park nearby, Potrero's McKinley Square, Mission Bay's waterfront parks, Crane Cove.
- Family: high in Bernal and Potrero; Mission Bay is wealthy but young and sends only ~15% of children to private school ([SF Chronicle via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/map-shows-f-neighborhoods-send-110019204.html)).
- Trade-offs: SoMa, Mission Bay and Dogpatch sit on bay fill (liquefaction zone) and in the sea-level-rise vulnerability zone ([search summary citing CGS](https://earthquakesafety.com/resource-seismic-maps), [Port — Mission Creek/Mission Bay resilience](https://www.sfport.com/wrp/mission-creek-mission-bay-resilience)); freeway noise/air along 101/280/Central Freeway; Mission street conditions vary widely by block; the former Mission Creek/Islais Creek corridors carry stormwater flood risk (check the SFPUC map).

### District 10 — Southeast
Neighborhoods: Bayview, Bayview Heights, Excelsior, Portola, Visitacion Valley, Silver Terrace, Mission Terrace, Crocker Amazon, Outer Mission.
- Character: the most affordable district for houses; working-class and immigrant families (Filipino, Chinese, Latino, Black communities); Portola's "garden district" history; Excelsior's Mission St corridor.
- Housing: 1900s–50s detached and semi-detached houses; some newer development at Hunters Point Shipyard/Candlestick.
- Price: $1.12M house median with the most sales of any district (452); condos $709K and slowest to sell (106 days) ([Team Kho](https://team-kho.com/san-francisco-housing-market-update-2026/)).
- Microclimate: generally sunny-to-mixed; the Excelsior/Crocker Amazon edge near the Daly City gap gets fog and wind.
- Transit: Balboa Park and Glen Park BART at the western edge; T Third through Bayview/Visitacion Valley; Bayshore Caltrain; 101 and 280 immediately adjacent.
- Parks: McLaren Park (city's second-largest), Candlestick Point.
- Family: high; Bayview private-school rate ~15% ([SF Chronicle via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/map-shows-f-neighborhoods-send-110019204.html)).
- Trade-offs: Bayview/Hunters Point includes fill and Islais Creek-area flood and sea-level exposure ([SF Planning — Islais Creek](https://sfplanning.org/project/islais)); some low-resource tracts are temporarily exempt from SB 79 upzoning until 2032 ([SF Planning SB 79](https://sfplanning.org/project/senate-bill-79-implementation-san-francisco)); industrial neighbors and freeway noise in parts; fewer walkable commercial corridors outside Mission St and San Bruno Ave.

## Schools (SFUSD)

Current system (2026–27): still the citywide choice lottery under Board Policy 5101. Families rank schools; about 80% get one of their top three ([SFUSD — Changes to Student Assignment](https://www.sfusd.edu/schools/enroll/student-assignment-policy/student-assignment-changes)). For oversubscribed attendance-area elementary schools the tiebreakers run: sibling → CTIP1 (lowest-test-score census tracts) → attendance area → none ([SFUSD tiebreakers](https://www.sfusd.edu/schools/enroll/student-assignment-policy/tiebreakers)). A new 2026–27 TK feeder system adds a one-year PK→TK tiebreaker at some early-education schools ([SFUSD TK feeder](https://www.sfusd.edu/TKFeeder)).

Zone-based system: adopted December 2020 as Board Policy 5101.2 — geographic zones drawn to mirror citywide demographics, each student guaranteed a school in their zone, assignment using neighborhood-based diversity categories ([SFUSD](https://www.sfusd.edu/schools/enroll/student-assignment-policy/student-assignment-changes)). Timeline has slipped repeatedly:
- Originally 2024–25, pushed to 2026–27 ([SFUSD, Oct 2022](https://www.sfusd.edu/announcements/2022-10-10-update-extending-student-assignment-policy-timeline-one-year-2026-27)).
- April 2025: not launching for 2026–27 ([SFUSD](https://www.sfusd.edu/schools/enroll/student-assignment-policy/student-assignment-changes)).
- March 2026: postponed indefinitely; zone map not finalized, tied to school closures ([SF Standard, Mar 2026](https://sfstandard.com/2026/03/16/sfusd-lottery-school-assignment-system/)).
- May 2026: the lottery stays for 2026–27 and 2027–28; the redesign would take effect no earlier than 2028–29, and closures slide to 2029–30 at the earliest. Simulations showed the adopted policy "cannot guarantee that students living within a zone would be assigned to a school there" ([SF Standard, May 2026](https://sfstandard.com/2026/05/18/sfusd-s-enrollment-overhaul-years-schedule-means-school-closures/)).

What buyers should take from this:
- Buying in a school's attendance area is a tiebreaker, not a guarantee — and it sits below CTIP1. At the most requested schools (Clarendon, Alvarado, Webster account for over half of all attendance-area "misses") it frequently doesn't get you in ([sfeducation substack](https://sfeducation.substack.com/p/what-new-public-data-tells-us-about)).
- Whatever zones eventually look like, they're designed for demographic diversity, not proximity, so "buy next to the good school" may matter even less later. Don't pay a school premium on the assumption of assignment.
- Most competitive programs are language immersion — Alvarado (Spanish), Alice Fong Yu (Cantonese), Jose Ortega (Mandarin) — plus West Portal and Lawton TK ([SF Standard, Mar 2025](https://sfstandard.com/2025/03/26/sf-public-schools-ranking-admissions/)). Alamo, Sunset, Clarendon and New Traditions K classes are also among the most popular ([sfeducation substack](https://sfeducation.substack.com/p/what-new-public-data-tells-us-about)).
- Private school is mainstream: roughly 30% of SF students attend private school — highest of any California county vs. 7.8% statewide; over 75% in Sea Cliff and the Marina, under 10% in Chinatown and the Tenderloin ([SF Chronicle, Oct 2025, via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/map-shows-f-neighborhoods-send-110019204.html)). Budget for it as a real scenario.
- Middle and high school: Lowell (academic) and SOTA (arts) are admissions-based citywide schools — confirm current admissions rules with SFUSD, as Lowell's policy has changed repeatedly.

## Hazards to check

- Liquefaction and earthquake-induced landslides: CGS Seismic Hazard Zones (state-mandated; triggers the Natural Hazard Disclosure) ([CGS](https://www.conservation.ca.gov/cgs/shma)); block-level lookup via CGS EQ Zapp; city layer on [DataSF](https://data.sf.gov/-/San-Francisco-Seismic-Hazard-Zones/7ahv-68ap). Liquefaction concentrates on historic bay fill — the Marina, Fisherman's Wharf, SoMa, Mission Bay, Dogpatch, parts of Bayview, Treasure Island. Landslide zones follow the hills and bluffs — Outer Richmond, Sea Cliff, Presidio, Lake Shore, Bayview Heights, Midtown Terrace, Twin Peaks, Clarendon Heights, Golden Gate Heights, Forest Hill, Diamond Heights, Castro, Dolores Heights, Noe Valley, Yerba Buena Island ([search summary of the SF seismic hazard layer](https://data.sf.gov/-/San-Francisco-Seismic-Hazard-Zones/7ahv-68ap)). Being in a zone isn't disqualifying — it means foundation/engineering diligence and possibly earthquake insurance cost.
- Stormwater flooding: SFPUC 100-Year Storm Flood Risk Map — areas with ≥6 inches of flooding over at least half a block in a 100-year storm; updated December 2024 to add flood elevations ([SFPUC Flood Maps](https://www.sfpuc.gov/learning/emergency-preparedness/flood-maps), [FAQ PDF](https://www.sfpuc.gov/sites/default/files/learning/SFPUC_100YearStormFloodRiskMap_InfoFAQs.pdf)). Sellers must disclose if the property is in the zone (Police Code Art. 51) ([SF Police Code §5102](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_police/0-0-0-51275)). Floodwater grants up to $100K for mitigation. This map does not cover bay/ocean flooding.
- Sea-level rise: ~4 square miles of SF (excluding Treasure Island and SFO) are in the city's SLR vulnerability zone (108 inches above today's high tide — an upper-range end-of-century scenario) ([SF Planning — Sea Level Rise](https://sfplanning.org/sea-level-rise-action-plan)). Mission Bay (Mission Creek), Islais Creek/Bayview and the Embarcadero are the focus; in August 2026 the Port and US Army Corps finalized a recommended plan for 7.5 miles of waterfront from Fisherman's Wharf to south of Islais Creek, designed for up to ~9 ft of rise by 2140, but funding faces hurdles ([SF Port](https://www.sfport.com/about/news/port-san-francisco-us-army-corps-engineers-release-draft-plan-build-citys-flood-defenses), [SF Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/climate/article/san-francisco-waterfront-plan-22387088.php)).
- Soft-story retrofit: mandatory for wood-frame buildings with 5+ units, 2+ stories over a soft/weak story, permitted before 1978; all compliance deadlines have passed and non-compliant buildings are placarded "Earthquake Warning" ([SF ESIP — Soft Story](https://www.sfgov.org/sfc/esip/soft-story)). ~94% compliance as of Feb 2025 (4,655 done, 284 non-compliant) ([SF News Today](https://sfnewstoday.com/news/san-francisco-soft-story-retrofit-grant-2026/)). For a condo/TIC in a 5+ unit wood building: confirm the retrofit permit is finaled (DBI) and ask whether a special assessment is still outstanding. Buildings under 5 units aren't covered — a garage-over-living-space 2–4 unit Edwardian may still need a voluntary retrofit.
- Air quality: SF's Air Pollutant Exposure Zone (Health Code Art. 38) marks areas near freeways and major sources; shown on the PIM's "Environmental Information" tab ([SF DPH Art. 38](https://www.sfdph.org/dph/eh/Air/Article38.asp), [SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/air-quality-community-risk-reduction-plan)).

## Tools for evaluating a block

- SF Planning Property Information Map (PIM) — zoning, height/bulk, permit and planning history, historic status, build year, assessed value, environmental layers ([PIM](https://sfplanninggis.org/pim/)). Also check "Permits in My Neighborhood" for upcoming projects nearby ([SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/resource/permits-my-neighborhood)).
- DBI Permit/Complaint Tracking — building, electrical, plumbing permits and complaints by address or block/lot; most complete for permits since Oct 2004 ([DBI PTS](https://dbiweb02.sfgov.org/dbipts/), [SF.gov](https://www.sfgov.org/services/building-permit-status)). Look for unpermitted work (a finished basement with no permit), open complaints, and whether a soft-story or remodel permit was finaled.
- 311 data — every 311 case since July 2008, updated nightly; filter to a block for encampment, graffiti, dumping, noise patterns ([DataSF 311 Cases](https://data.sfgov.org/City-Infrastructure/311-Cases/vw6y-z8j6), [SF.gov neighborhood map](https://www.sf.gov/data--311-service-requests-neighborhood)).
- Crime — SFPD Incident Reports 2018–present on DataSF, updated daily ([dataset explainer](https://sfdigitalservices.gitbook.io/dataset-explainers/sfpd-incident-report-2018-to-present)); SFPD Crime Dashboard for Part I year-over-year ([SFPD](https://www.sanfranciscopolice.org/stay-safe/crime-data/crime-dashboard)). Compare rates, not raw counts, and expect car break-ins to dominate property crime everywhere.
- Walk Score / Transit Score ([Walk Score SF](https://www.walkscore.com/CA/San_Francisco)).
- Hazards: CGS EQ Zapp, SFPUC flood map, Art. 38 zone on the PIM (above).
- Noise: freeway and Muni/Caltrain proximity, Market/Mission/Van Ness bus corridors, bar streets (Valencia, Polk, Chestnut, Union) — visit on a Friday night and a weekday rush hour.

## Zoning context: Family Zoning Plan and SB 79

- Family Zoning Plan: passed by the Board of Supervisors 7–4 on Dec 2, 2025; signed Dec 12, 2025; effective Jan 12, 2026 ([SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/sf-family-zoning-plan), [SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2025/12/02/family-zoning-supervisors-vote-daniel-lurie/)). It targets the west and north ("Housing Opportunity Areas") and deliberately skips eastern/southern neighborhoods rezoned in earlier plans ([KQED explainer](https://www.kqed.org/news/12065204/everything-you-need-to-know-about-san-franciscos-family-zoning-plan)). About 96,000 parcels affected.
  - Commercial/transit corridors: mid-rise (6–8 stories) and some high-rise (9+); 65 ft on California, Clement and Balboa; taller on parts of Lombard and Van Ness; developers can build up to 6–8 stories on most commercial/transit streets if the new building adds at least one unit ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2025/12/02/family-zoning-supervisors-vote-daniel-lurie/), [SF Chronicle map](https://www.sfchronicle.com/projects/2025/sf-family-zoning-plan-map/)).
  - Residential blocks: height limits mostly unchanged (~40 ft / 4 stories), but density caps removed — duplexes through fourplexes plus ADUs allowed; up to 5 stories near commercial streets; up to 6 on corner lots and sites over 8,000 sq ft ([SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/sf-family-zoning-plan)).
  - Protections: rent-controlled buildings with 3+ units and landmarks are excluded from upzoning ([KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12065204/everything-you-need-to-know-about-san-franciscos-family-zoning-plan)).
  - Litigation: neighborhood and small-business groups have sued on CEQA grounds; outcome pending as of the last report found ([The Real Deal, Mar 2026](https://therealdeal.com/san-francisco/2026/03/04/lawsuits-could-trigger-blunter-upzoning-in-san-francisco/)).
- SB 79 (state transit-oriented upzoning, effective July 2026): SF adopted an Alternative Plan ordinance signed May 8, 2026, rather than accepting state defaults. It covers ~120,000 parcels near ~270 qualifying stops in two tiers (BART/Caltrain; Muni light rail/BRT); ~19,850 parcels in low-resource tracts are exempt until 2032 ([SF Planning — SB 79](https://sfplanning.org/project/senate-bill-79-implementation-san-francisco)). State defaults would allow 55–95 ft near transit; the city's plan keeps ~40 ft caps in the more residential areas of the Richmond and Sunset ([The Real Deal via search summary](https://therealdeal.com/san-francisco/2026/03/04/lawsuits-could-trigger-blunter-upzoning-in-san-francisco/)).

What this means for a buyer near a corridor (Geary, Clement, California, Irving, Judah, Taraval, Noriega, Lombard, Van Ness, Union, Fillmore, West Portal Ave, etc.):
- The lot next door or across the back fence could become a 6–8 story building. Look the neighbours up on the PIM, not just the subject property. Sun, views and privacy you're paying for may not last.
- Upside: more nearby retail and density; small older commercial parcels may gain development value.
- A single-family lot on a residential block can now legally hold more units within the existing height — an option value, not a sure thing, given construction costs.
- The city's own economists expect far fewer units than the zoning allows (Planning estimates ~19,000; the Chief Economist ~14,600) ([KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12065204/everything-you-need-to-know-about-san-franciscos-family-zoning-plan)), so change will be spotty and slow rather than wholesale.

## Other buyer notes (general knowledge — verify before quoting)

- TICs (tenancies-in-common) are common in 2–6 unit buildings: cheaper than condos but come with fractional loans from a small set of lenders and co-owner agreements; condo conversion is restricted.
- Rent control applies to most pre-June 1979 multi-unit buildings ([SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/sf-family-zoning-plan)); buying a tenant-occupied unit to live in involves owner-move-in rules.
- Older housing stock means diligence on foundations, knob-and-tube wiring, sewer laterals and unpermitted work is routine.
