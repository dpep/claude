# Buying in San Francisco

Reference for the `realtor` persona: how a purchase works in SF and California — offers, representation, forms, costs, property types, financing. As of September 2026. Every claim is tagged:

- **[D]** documented — statute, government agency, C.A.R./NAR, or a law firm quoting the statute/form
- **[P]** practitioner — agent/lender/contractor blogs, market reports; real but self-interested or anecdotal
- **[?]** conflicting or unverified — sources disagree, or only a weak source; verify before stating as fact

Numbers that move (rates, limits, fees, market stats) are dated. Treat them as "as of" snapshots, not constants.

## 1. The SF offer process

### Pre-marketed disclosure packets
- In SF most due diligence happens *before* contract: the seller commissions and pays for inspection reports and puts them in the disclosure package during marketing. [P] ([Seb Frey](https://sebfrey.com/non-contingent-offers-in-bay-area-home-sales/), [Wynne Morgensen](https://wynnemorgensen.com/blog/the-san-francisco-disclosure-package-explained))
- Typical packet: TDS, SPQ, AVID, NHD, lead-paint disclosure (pre-1978), pest/WDO report (Section 1/2), preliminary title report, permit history; often a general home inspection; sometimes roof, sewer lateral, foundation reports; for condos, HOA docs (CC&Rs, budget, reserves, litigation). [P] ([Wynne Morgensen](https://wynnemorgensen.com/blog/the-san-francisco-disclosure-package-explained))
- **3R report** (Report of Residential Building Record) is SF-specific: "Owners or realtors are legally required to provide the buyer with a 3R report." It shows *building* permit history only — not plumbing, electrical, or commercial permits. Fee $379 per building (DBI schedule effective July 2026), 7–10 business days. [D] ([SF.gov – 3R](https://www.sf.gov/request3r)) The exact code section cited varies across sources [?].
- Buyers commonly still do their own pre-offer inspection or contractor walkthrough on top of the seller's reports. [P]

### Offer dates, underpricing, over-asking
- Listing agent sets an offer review date ~5–7 days after going live; all buyers submit best-and-final simultaneously; "there usually is no second chance." [P] ([Helena 7x7](https://www.helena7x7.com/sf-buyers-playbook-competitive-market/))
- **Strategic underpricing** (list price as bait): long-standing SF practice. SocketSite's standing critique: "sold over asking" only means something if the list price tried to estimate value, which in SF it rarely does. [P] ([SocketSite](https://socketsite.com/archives/2024/05/at-asking-sale-yields-nearly-30-percent-less.html)) Agents defend it as "competitive pricing." [P] ([The Front Steps, 2014](https://thefrontsteps.com/2014/06/02/are-overbids-a-result-of-intentional-underpricing-no-its-competitive-pricing/))
- **Over-asking by segment (2026 market data):** [P, market reports]
  - Aug 2026 SFH: 124.1% of list on average; 86.4% sold over asking; median $1.855M; 13 DOM. [P] ([McHale / Vanguard data](https://www.markdmchale.com/blog/san-francisco-real-estate-market-report-september-2026))
  - Aug 2026 condo/TIC/co-op: 106.3% of list; 53.5% over asking (up from 28.6%); median $1.225M; 14 DOM. [P] (same)
  - Apr 2026: SFH ~23% over original list, condos ~7%. [P] ([The Front Steps](https://thefrontsteps.com/2026/04/22/april2026-market-update/))
  - H1 2026: 144 SF homes sold ≥$1M over asking vs 8 in H1 2025. [P] ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/07/07/sf-hyper-bidding-home-sales/), [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/san-francisco/2026/07/07/144-san-francisco-homes-sell-for-1-million-over-asking/))
  - [?] Condo narrative conflicts: some agent blogs say high-rise/SoMa condos still trade at or slightly above list, 8–12% below 2022 peak, with 4–5 months of supply ([Robyn Kaufman](https://sfhighrises.com/san-francisco-real-estate-in-2026-looks-promising-for-highrise-condo-buyers), [rises.co](https://rises.co/blog/soma-condos-and-lofts-how-the-market-really-performs)), while the August stats show condos accelerating fast. The honest version: SFH are hot citywide; condo results depend on building type (high-rise with big HOA dues vs. boutique/Edwardian flats) and are rising.
- Rule of thumb: never anchor a buyer on list price; anchor on recent comps for that micro-market and segment.

### Pre-emptive offers
- Offer made before the offer date (sometimes before MLS), usually strong on price, often all-cash or non-contingent, with a short expiration to force a decision. [P] ([Legacy Real Estate](https://legacysfhomes.com/blog/preemptive-offers))
- Listing agents must present all offers to the seller (agent duty). [P] (same) Many listings state "seller reserves the right to accept a pre-emptive offer" — or explicitly won't review early. [P]

### Contingencies: non-contingent as the norm
- In competitive Bay Area deals, offers waiving inspection/loan/appraisal contingencies have been normal "for the past few years"; sellers with full disclosure packets expect "clean" offers. [P] ([Seb Frey](https://sebfrey.com/non-contingent-offers-in-bay-area-home-sales/))
- The risk: once contingencies are waived, a buyer who can't close typically loses the deposit as liquidated damages. [D-ish, law firm] ([Tyler Law](https://www.tylerlawllp.com/blog-posts/winning-bidding-wars-with-non-contingent-offers))
- In softer segments (many condos) contingent offers still get accepted. [P]

### Escalation clauses
- Exist, but reveal your ceiling; SF's sealed-bid offer-date format means they're uncommon, and many listing agents disregard them. [P/?] ([Helena 7x7](https://www.helena7x7.com/sf-buyers-playbook-competitive-market/)) I found no SF data on how often they're used — treat "rare in SF" as practitioner folklore. Listing agents more commonly run a second round ("highest and best") among the top offers. [P]

### Love letters
- No California statute bans them. [P, citing absence of law] NAR discourages them and recommends agents not draft/deliver them; they risk fair-housing violations (federal FHA, California FEHA) if a seller picks based on protected characteristics. Oregon's 2021 ban was struck down on First Amendment grounds. [D/P] ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/law-and-ethics/first-amendment-argument-wins-the-day), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2021/10/03/1042802570/oregon-bans-homebuyer-love-letters-for-potential-fair-housing-law-violations), [Brian Cooper](https://cooperfamilyrealestate.com/blog/personal-letter-to-seller-california-fair-housing-risks))
- Stance: discourage; if the client insists, strip anything revealing a protected class (family status, religion, national origin, etc.).

### Deposit and timeline
- Deposit ("EMD") typically 1–3% in SF, usually at the top of that range at higher prices. [P] ([Makras](https://makrasrealestate.com/blog/how-escrow-works-in-san-francisco), [Aimee Tenenté](https://aimeetenenterealestate.com/blog/how-escrow-works-in-san-francisco)) 3% is also the statutory liquidated-damages ceiling for a 1–4 unit owner-occupied home (Civ. Code §1675) — which is why "3%" is the reflexive number. [D, statute; verify wording before quoting]
- RPA default: deposit delivered within 3 business days of acceptance (wire). [P/D] ([Cooper](https://cooperfamilyrealestate.com/blog/california-contingency-removal-timeline-day-by-day))
- Escrow length: financed ~21–45 days (30 common); cash 7–21 days. [P] ([Ruth Krishnan](https://ruthkrishnan.com/the-escrow-process/), [Makras](https://makrasrealestate.com/blog/how-escrow-works-in-san-francisco)) Sellers may prefer a faster close over a slightly higher price. [P]

## 2. Post-NAR-settlement buyer representation

### NAR settlement (effective Aug 17, 2024)
- MLS participants working with a buyer must have a **written agreement before touring** a home (in person or live virtual). [D] ([NAR – Written Buyer Agreements 101](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/written-buyer-agreements-101))
- The compensation in that agreement must be "objectively ascertainable and not open-ended" — no "whatever the seller offers." [D] ([NAR settlement FAQs](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/nar-settlement-faqs), [Florida Realtors FAQ](https://www.floridarealtors.org/law-ethics/nar-settlement-faqs))
- No agreement needed to walk into an open house or just talk to an agent about services. [D] ([NAR consumer guide](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-to-open-houses-and-written-agreements))
- **Offers of buyer-broker compensation cannot appear in the MLS** (including private remarks/linked sites). Sellers may still offer it, off-MLS. SFAR MLS can demand a copy of the buyer agreement within one business day. [D] ([SFAR guide](https://sfar.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/69000857634-guide-to-changes-for-nar-settlement))

### California law layered on top: AB 2992 (effective Jan 1, 2025)
- Written buyer-broker agreement required "as soon as practicable, but no later than the execution of the buyer's offer." [D] ([Tyler Law](https://www.tylerlawllp.com/blog-posts/new-laws-2025-special-edition-mandatory-buyer-broker-agreements))
- Max term **3 months (90 days)** for individual buyers; renewals must be written and signed; no auto-renewal. Must state compensation amount, services, when it's due, termination, and a conspicuous notice that compensation is negotiable and not set by law. [D] (same; [Brownstein](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/california-s-new-requirements-for-buyer-broker-representation-agreements/))
- So in CA: NAR rule → sign before touring; state law → sign before offer at the latest.

### C.A.R. forms
- **BRBC** (Buyer Representation and Broker Compensation Agreement) is the standard CA form. [P] ([Seb Frey](https://sebfrey.com/the-brbc-form-buyer-representation-and-broker-compensation-agreement-what-bay-area-buyers-need-to-know/)) C.A.R. also offers a lighter touring-only agreement for buyers unwilling to sign a full BRBC. [P] ([RHRC](https://www.rhrc.net/c-a-r-s-solution-for-buyers-who-want-to-tour-a-property-but-are-unwilling-to-sign-a-brbc/)) [?] exact form name not confirmed here.
- RPA changes: a paragraph (3G(3)) where the buyer states a buyer-broker agreement exists and records any seller-paid buyer-broker compensation; buyer's broker becomes a third-party beneficiary able to enforce it; escrow provides each side a statement of what its broker received. [D, law firm summary] ([Tyler Law](https://www.tylerlawllp.com/blog-posts/c-a-r-updated-and-new-forms-coming-in-2025))

### How compensation is negotiated now, and what's typical in SF
- Mechanics: buyer's agreement sets the fee; the offer asks the seller to pay some/all of it (as a term of the RPA); buyer pays any shortfall. [D/P]
- Nationally, buyer-agent commission hasn't dropped much: ~2.4% Q1 2025 vs 2.36% Q3 2024. [P] ([The MortgagePoint](https://themortgagepoint.com/2025/05/16/measuring-the-impact-of-nar-settlements-on-agent-commissions/))
- SF: agent sources say the large majority of sellers still cover buyer-agent fees (one cites ~94% in the Bay Area); in bidding wars some buyers absorb part of their fee to strengthen the offer. [P/?] ([Lisa V SF](https://lisavsfrealestate.com/blog/nar-settlement-and-what-it-means-for-buyers-and-sellers)) Not independently verified. Commonly quoted SF buyer-side range is ~2–2.5%. [P]

## 3. California forms, contingencies, disclosures

### RPA (C.A.R. Residential Purchase Agreement)
- Current generation dated 12/21, revised 12/22 and 12/24 (and later form releases). [P]
- **Default contingency periods:** investigation 17 days; appraisal 17 days; loan 17 or 21 days. [?] Sources disagree: some cite 21 days for the loan contingency ([Cooper](https://cooperfamilyrealestate.com/blog/california-contingency-removal-timeline-day-by-day)), which was the 2014–2021 RPA-CA default ([Fehlman](https://fehlmanlaw.com/real-estate-law-tips/page/4/)); others say 17 ([Tyler Law](https://www.tylerlawllp.com/blog-posts/winning-bidding-wars-with-non-contingent-offers)). Check the actual form in use. All are negotiable, and in SF often shortened or waived.
- Contingencies are removed **in writing** (C.A.R. contingency-removal form); they don't lapse automatically. If the buyer is late, the seller must first serve a Notice to Buyer to Perform before cancelling. [P/D] ([Tyler Law](https://www.tylerlawllp.com/blog-posts/winning-bidding-wars-with-non-contingent-offers))

### Seller disclosures
- **TDS** (Transfer Disclosure Statement, Civ. Code §1102 et seq.) — known material defects. [D]
- **SPQ** (Seller Property Questionnaire, C.A.R.) — repairs, unpermitted work, insurance claims, neighborhood issues; a contractual form. [P/D] ([Cooper](https://cooperfamilyrealestate.com/california-seller-disclosure-forms-tds-spq-nhd-megans-law))
- **AVID** (Agent Visual Inspection Disclosure) — the agent's own reasonably diligent visual inspection (Civ. Code §2079, 1–4 units). [D]
- **NHD** (Natural Hazard Disclosure, Civ. Code §1103) — third-party report on state-mapped zones (flood, fire, fault, seismic/liquefaction, dam inundation). [D] ([Brittani summary](https://brittani.ai/blog/california-seller-disclosure-requirements-2026/))
- **Lead-based paint** (pre-1978): federal disclosure + EPA pamphlet; buyer gets a 10-day testing opportunity unless waived. [D]
- **Earthquake:** for 1–4 unit light-frame dwellings built before 1960, seller must provide the *Homeowner's Guide to Earthquake Safety* and an earthquake-hazards disclosure (Gov. Code §8897.1). [D] ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-gov/title-2/division-1/chapter-13-8/article-2/section-8897-1/), [Seismic Safety Commission](https://ssc.ca.gov/forms_pubs/hog/)) The combined C.A.R. booklet also covers environmental hazards. [P]
- Also: Megan's Law notice, Mello-Roos/special assessments, deaths on property within 3 years. [D] ([Brittani](https://brittani.ai/blog/california-seller-disclosure-requirements-2026/))

### Buyer's right to cancel after late disclosure
- If the TDS is delivered after the purchase agreement is signed, the buyer may terminate within **3 days after in-person delivery or 5 days after mail/electronic delivery** (Civ. Code §1102.3). [D] ([Justia §1102.3](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-2/part-4/title-4/chapter-2/article-1-5/section-1102-3/))
- The RPA separately gives a review period for disclosures delivered during escrow. [?] Check the exact number of days on the form.

### Point-of-sale safety items
- Smoke alarm required at sale of a single-family dwelling (H&S Code §13113.8). [D] CO detectors required where there's a fossil-fuel appliance, fireplace, or attached garage (H&S §17926). [D] ([Justia §17926](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-hsc/division-13/part-1-5/chapter-2/section-17926/))
- Water-heater bracing is also a statutory point-of-sale item (H&S §19211). [D, from general knowledge; not re-verified here]

## 4. Costs

### SF transfer tax (Business & Tax Regs Code Art. 12C)
The rate applies to the **whole price**, not marginally. [P] ([Burgelman](https://burgelmanhomes.com/blog/san-francisco-transfer-tax-rates-credits-and-who-pays))

| Price | Per $500 | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| $100–$250,000 | $2.50 | 0.50% |
| $250,001–$999,999 | $3.40 | 0.68% |
| $1M–$4,999,999 | $3.75 | 0.75% |
| $5M–$9,999,999 | $11.25 | 2.25% |
| $10M–$24,999,999 | $27.50 | 5.50% |
| $25M+ | $30.00 | 6.00% |

[P] Table from agent sites ([Burgelman](https://burgelmanhomes.com/blog/san-francisco-transfer-tax-rates-credits-and-who-pays)); the 5.5%/6% top tiers are confirmed by [SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2025/12/16/san-francisco-transfer-tax-reform/) and [SPUR](https://www.spur.org/voter-guide/2020-11/sf-prop-i-transfer-tax-increase). I couldn't load the Assessor-Recorder's own rate table; confirm there.

- **Correction to the brief:** the $10M+ doubling was **Prop I of November 2020**, not 2024. [D] ([SPUR](https://www.spur.org/voter-guide/2020-11/sf-prop-i-transfer-tax-increase))
- **Prop C (2024)** lets the Board of Supervisors reduce or suspend transfer tax without a vote; a Prop I repeal and a "BUILD Act" cut for $10M+ commercial/multifamily were floated, and the BUILD Act was paused in June 2026. [P/D] ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2025/12/16/san-francisco-transfer-tax-reform/), [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/3/san-franciscos-build-act-proposed-transfer-tax-reductions-to-stimulate-housing-and-commercial-development))
- **Nov 2026 ballot, new "Prop I" (Affordable Housing Guarantee Act):** would dedicate 50% of existing $10M+ transfer-tax revenue to affordable/social housing and rental assistance. It does **not** change rates. [D, news] ([Local News Matters](https://localnewsmatters.org/2026/09/25/san-francisco-prop-i-affordable-housing/))
- Irrelevant to most homebuyers: $10M+ residential is rare. The tier that matters is $1M–$5M at 0.75%, e.g. $15,000 on a $2M sale.

### Who pays what (SF custom — negotiable)
- **Transfer tax:** seller, by custom. [P] ([Burgelman](https://burgelmanhomes.com/blog/san-francisco-transfer-tax-rates-credits-and-who-pays), title-company guides)
- **Escrow fee and owner's title policy:** buyer, by custom in SF (unlike the 50/50 escrow split elsewhere in CA). [P] ([Susanne Alexander](https://susannealexander.com/blog/san-francisco-closing-costs-explained-for-buyers), [Legacy](https://legacysfhomes.com/blog/a-guide-to-california-closing-costs)) Title-company county charts (ORT, Fidelity, Chicago Title) are the authoritative reference, but I couldn't parse the PDFs. [?]

### Property tax
- Prop 13: 1% base on assessed value (reset to purchase price at sale), +≤2%/yr. FY2025-26 SF secured rate: **1.18268325%** including voter-approved bonds. [D] ([SF Treasurer](https://sftreasurer.org/property/secured-property-taxes))
- Parcel taxes on top (FY2025-26, per parcel): School Parcel Tax of 2020 $334.38; SFUSD Teacher Support $319.34; SFUSD Facilities District $43 per SFH ($21.50 per unit multifamily); City College $99; SF Bay Restoration Authority $12. Roughly **$800/yr** fixed, plus any CBD/CFD special assessments. Seniors can exempt some. [D] ([SF Treasurer – parcel taxes](https://sftreasurer.org/property/parcel-tax-exemptions-and-special-assessments))
- Rule of thumb for buyers: ~1.18% of price + ~$800/yr.
- **Supplemental tax bill:** after a sale the Assessor reassesses and issues a prorated supplemental bill for the difference between the new and prior value. It comes after closing, often months later, is **not** paid through escrow or impounds, and surprises first-time buyers. [D] ([SF Treasurer](https://sftreasurer.org/property/secured-property-taxes))
- Installments go delinquent after Dec 10 and Apr 10. [D] (same)
- Homeowners' exemption: $7,000 off assessed value (~$80/yr); file by Feb 15 for the full amount. [D] ([SF.gov](https://www.sf.gov/file-homeowners-exemption))
- Prop 19 (2021): 55+, severely disabled, or disaster victims can carry their base-year value to a replacement home anywhere in CA. [D] ([SF.gov](https://www.sf.gov/transfer-your-property-tax-base-replacement-home), [BOE](https://www.boe.ca.gov/pdf/pub801.pdf))

### HOA dues, special assessments
- Agent-reported: SF HOA fees rose ~26% 2019–2024, driven by insurance premiums and SB 326; luxury high-rises run $1,200–$3,500+/mo. [P] ([Ruth Krishnan](https://ruthkrishnan.com/san-francisco-condo-prices-rising-2026/), [SF Highrises](https://sfhighrises.com/san-francisco-real-estate-in-2026-looks-promising-for-highrise-condo-buyers))
- **SB 326 (Civ. Code §5551):** condo HOAs must have a licensed engineer/architect inspect exterior elevated elements (balconies, decks, walkways) on a 9-year cycle; first deadline Jan 1, 2025 (not extended — the extension was for apartments under SB 721). [D, law firm] ([Bay Legal](https://baylegal.com/sb-326-for-hoas-condominium-balcony-inspection-requirements-after-january-2026/))
- Balcony findings are producing large special assessments; one source cites $40k–$175k per unit. [P/?] ([Dr. Balcony](https://drbalcony.com/what-is-the-california-senate-bill-326/)) A claim that "SB 410" puts the report into buyer disclosure packets from 2026 is **unverified**. [?]
- Buyer checklist: reserve study (% funded), recent/planned special assessments, board minutes, litigation, master insurance policy (coverage and deductible), earthquake coverage on the master policy, % owner-occupied, and lender warrantability.

### Insurance (CA/SF-specific)
- The statewide market is in turmoil: State Farm got an emergency 17% interim homeowners increase (settled March 2026); CA average premiums forecast +16% in 2026; FAIR Plan >600k policies, filed +35.8%; the "Sustainable Insurance Strategy" trades rate approvals for commitments to write in high-risk areas. [P/D, news] ([Insurify](https://insurify.com/homeowners-insurance/news/2026-california-home-insurance-report/), [Latent](https://www.latentinsure.com/blog/california-homeowners-insurance-news))
- SF is mostly **not** a wildfire story. The SF problem is old-home systems: many admitted carriers decline homes with **active knob-and-tube wiring**; surplus-lines carriers may write at 2–3× with a replace-within-6–12-months condition; FAIR Plan is the last resort (limited coverage, no liability — pair with a DIC policy). [P] ([Marindependent](https://marindependent.com/knob-and-tube-is-now-uninsurable/), [Honeycomb](https://honeycombinsurance.com/insurance-learning-center/knob-and-tube-wiring)) Advice: **get an insurance quote during the contingency period (or before a non-contingent offer)**. Lenders require proof of insurance to fund.
- **Earthquake:** standard homeowners policies exclude it. The CEA sells deductibles of 5/10/15/20/25%; homes with >$1M dwelling coverage, or pre-1980 raised-foundation homes without a verified retrofit, are limited to 15–25%. Retrofit discounts up to 25%. [D] ([CEA](https://www.earthquakeauthority.com/california-earthquake-insurance-policies/homeowners/coverages-and-deductibles)) SF premium estimates of $2k–$5k/yr come from aggregator sites. [P/?] Condo owners also need to know whether the HOA master policy carries earthquake coverage (loss-assessment coverage).

## 5. Property types

### SFH vs condo vs TIC vs co-op
- **Condo:** separate legal parcel, conventional financing (subject to project warrantability), HOA under Davis-Stirling.
- **TIC (tenancy in common):** you own an undivided % of the whole building, plus a TIC agreement granting exclusive use of a unit. Common in SF because condo conversion is restricted. [D/P]
  - **Fractional TIC loans** (each owner has a separate loan) come from a handful of mostly local lenders: Sterling Bank & Trust, Bank of Marin, Redwood CU, Patelco, Meriwest, NCB and others. Mostly limited to SF and Marin. [P] ([Andy Sirkin](https://andysirkin.com/tenancy-in-common-tic/essentials-for-buyers-and-their-realtors/getting-a-tic-mortgage-loan/), [Inside SF RE](https://insidesfre.com/more-tic-lender-choices-and-decent-interest-rates/), [Redwood CU](https://www.redwoodcu.org/tic-loans/), [Bank of Marin](https://www.bankofmarin.com/products-and-services/lending/tenants-in-common-tic-loans/))
  - Terms: max LTV ~70–80% (so 20–30%+ down); rates ~0.5–1% above condo loans; mostly 3/5/7-year fixed-then-adjustable, and historically no 30-year fixed. [P] (Sirkin; the lender list is a moving target [?])
  - **Group loans** (one blanket mortgage) carry co-borrower default risk and complicate resale. Avoid unless the price reflects it. [P] (Sirkin)
  - TIC trades at a discount to an equivalent condo. [P]
- **Co-op:** shares plus a proprietary lease; share loan (NCB is the main lender); 20–50% down common; board approval takes 2–6 weeks and the board can reject any buyer for any non-discriminatory reason. Concentrated in Nob Hill, Russian Hill, Pacific Heights. [P] ([McMullen](https://mcmullen.properties/blog/buying-a-co-op-in-san-francisco-what-you-need-to-know), [NCB](https://www.ncb.coop/personal-banking/lending/mortgage-loans))

### Condo conversion (TIC → condo)
- The annual **condo conversion lottery is suspended**. It was paused when the Expedited Conversion Program (2013) let queued TICs convert for a fee. Resumption was "expected 2024, 2025 or 2026" and **still hasn't happened as of Sept 2026**. [P, attorney] ([Andy Sirkin](https://andysirkin.com/subdivision-and-condominium-conversion/condominium-conversion-eligibility/summary-san-francisco-condominium-conversion-rules/), [LA Metro Home Finder](https://www.lametrohomefinder.com/blog/condo-conversion-subdivision-san-francisco-2026)) [?] on the exact current status; check with SF Public Works / a TIC attorney.
- When the lottery returns: 2–4 unit buildings only; all but one unit owner-occupied for 3 years; each owner ≥10%; up to 200 units/yr. [P, attorney] (Sirkin)
- **2-unit owner-occupied bypass:** both units owner-occupied (each owner ≥25%, principal residence ≥1 year) → convert without the lottery. Still available. [P, attorney] (Sirkin) Cost ~$20–25k in fees plus any required building work. [P]
- **Eviction history can kill conversion:** a no-fault eviction of a senior/disabled tenant disqualifies the building; 2+ no-fault evictions extend the owner-occupancy requirement to 10 years; no lottery entry for 8 years after the most recent no-fault eviction. [P, attorney] (Sirkin) **Buyouts** also count: since Oct 31, 2014, a buyout of a senior/disabled/catastrophically-ill tenant, or 2+ other buyouts within 10 years, bars conversion — and the bar runs with the building to later owners. [D] ([Admin Code §37.9E](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_admin/0-0-0-49496), [SF.gov buyouts](https://www.sf.gov/information--buyout-agreements))
- Diligence on any TIC or 2–4 unit building: Rent Board eviction and buyout history ("Ellis history"). An Ellis Act withdrawal can restrict re-rental and conversion for years. [D/P] ([SFTU](https://sftu.org/ellis/), [Sirkin](https://andysirkin.com/tenancy-in-common-tic/san-francisco-rent-control-and-tenants-rights/owner-occupancy-and-ellis-act-evictions/))

### In-law / unwarranted units, ADUs
- **Dwelling Unit Legalization Program:** a permanent (not amnesty) program to legalize existing unwarranted units, with Planning Code waivers (density, rear yard, open space, exposure, parking). All life-safety code requirements must be met. Starts at DBI. Legalized units **cannot be subdivided/sold separately**. Units that were rent-controlled stay rent-controlled after legalization. [D] ([SF Planning FAQ](https://sfplanning.org/resource/dwelling-unit-legalization-program-faq))
- Units ineligible for legalization may qualify under the ADU program. [D] ([SF Planning ADUs](https://sfplanning.org/accessory-dwelling-units))
- Removing an unwarranted unit can require Planning Commission Conditional Use authorization (Planning Code §317). [D] (SF Planning FAQ) Practitioner corollary: an unpermitted in-law you "plan to rip out" may not be removable. [P]
- A tenant living in an unwarranted unit generally has Rent Ordinance protections (an unpermitted unit doesn't escape just-cause rules). [P/?] Confirm with counsel.

### 2–4 unit buildings and rent control
- **SF Rent Ordinance** covers most multi-unit buildings with a certificate of occupancy **before June 13, 1979**. [P, consistent across sources] ([ZFC law](https://www.zfcpc.com/is-my-single-family-home-or-condo-in-san-francisco-exempt-from-rent-control/), [RentCheckMe](https://rentcheckme.com/articles/rent-control-in-san-francisco))
- **Costa-Hawkins (1995):** exempts separately alienable units (SFHs, condos) and post-Feb 1995 new construction from rent *caps*. But condos only escape if sold by the subdivider to a bona fide purchaser (post-2002 rule); tenancies that followed a no-fault eviction can stay controlled; **just-cause eviction still applies**. [D, law firm] ([Costa-Hawkins.com](https://costa-hawkins.com/qa/q-a-is-my-san-francisco-condominium-subject-to-rent-control/), [ZFC](https://www.zfcpc.com/is-my-single-family-home-or-condo-in-san-francisco-exempt-from-rent-control/))
- Allowable annual increase Mar 2026–Feb 2027: **1.6%**. [D] ([SF.gov rates](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit))
- **Owner move-in (OMI):** [D] ([SF.gov OMI](https://www.sf.gov/information--evictions-based-owner-or-relative-move), [SFTU](https://sftu.org/omi/))
  - Owner needs ≥25% interest (10% if ownership was recorded on/before Feb 21, 1991).
  - Owner or qualifying relative must move in within 3 months and occupy for **36 continuous months** as principal residence; notice includes a good-faith declaration under penalty of perjury; statement of occupancy filed with the Rent Board, updated every 90 days.
  - **One OMI per building**; the first OMI unit becomes the designated owner unit.
  - Protected tenants who can't be OMI'd: age 60+ or disabled with ≥10 years tenancy; catastrophically ill with ≥5 years; no OMI during the school year for households with minor children or school employees and ≥12 months tenancy.
  - Relocation (notices Mar 1, 2026–Feb 28, 2027): $8,245 per tenant, max $24,733 per unit, +$5,497 per elderly/disabled/household with minors. Ellis relocation: $11,110.05 per tenant, max $33,330.13 per unit, +$7,443.90 per elderly (62+)/disabled. [D] ([SF.gov rates](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit))
- **Buying tenant-occupied:** price reflects the tenant's rent, not market. Get tenant estoppel certificates (rent, deposit, term, side deals), Rent Board petitions/eviction/buyout history, and a lawyer's read on whether the unit you want can be OMI'd (a protected tenant can make it impossible). Buyouts need a Rent Board pre-buyout disclosure form, are filed publicly, and are rescindable by the tenant for 45 days. [D] ([SF.gov buyouts](https://www.sf.gov/information--buyout-agreements))

## 6. SF-specific gotchas

- **Soft-story retrofit (mandatory program, 2013):** wood-frame, 3+ stories (or 2 over basement/crawlspace), 5+ units, permitted before 1978. All deadlines have passed; as of the Dec 2024 DBI report, 4,648 of 4,941 buildings had complied and 293 had not (Feb 2025: 284). Non-compliant buildings carry NOVs and complicate sale and financing. [D, city data via secondary] ([SF.gov soft story](https://www.sfgov.org/sfc/esip/soft-story), [LA Metro Home Finder](https://www.lametrohomefinder.com/blog/soft-story-retrofit-san-francisco-2026)) Mostly relevant to 5+ unit buildings, i.e. larger TIC/condo buildings.
- **Sewer lateral:** SF has **no point-of-sale lateral certificate and no required inspection**. The owner owns the whole lateral; the owner repairs building-to-curb; SFPUC repairs the lower lateral (curb-to-main) for structural defects when reported via 311. Contrast South San Francisco and many East Bay/Peninsula cities, which do require it at sale. [P] ([Otter Squad](https://ottersquad.com/sewer-lateral-rules-san-francisco/)) [?] Confirm with SFPUC. A camera scope (~a few hundred dollars) is still standard diligence. [P]
- **Permit history / unpermitted work:** 3R report (building permits only) plus DBI online permit and complaint lookup, separate electrical/plumbing permits, Planning records. Unpermitted additions (garage bedrooms, attic/basement rooms) are common; the SPQ asks about them. [D/P] ([SF.gov 3R](https://www.sf.gov/request3r), [DBI records](https://www.sf.gov/departments--department-building-inspection--records-management-dbi))
- **Knob-and-tube:** see insurance above. Common in pre-1940 housing stock. [P]
- **Galvanized plumbing:** frequently flagged in older SF homes (reduced flow, corrosion, eventual replacement). [P] ([Burgelman pre-listing checklist](https://burgelmanhomes.com/blog/san-francisco-pre-listing-inspection-checklist)) No regulatory requirement found; practitioner item.
- **Foundations:** brick masonry foundations were standard before the 1910s; unreinforced brick performs poorly in earthquakes; CEA limits deductible options on unretrofitted pre-1980 raised foundations. [P/D] ([Bay Area Retrofit](https://bayarearetrofit.com/brick-foundations/), [Inside SF RE](https://insidesfre.com/should-you-buy-a-property-that-has-a-brick-foundation/), [CEA](https://www.earthquakeauthority.com/california-earthquake-insurance-policies/homeowners/coverages-and-deductibles)) Contractor-quoted replacement costs of $80k–$180k (one says ~$160k average in SF) are **contractor marketing**. [P/?] Recommend a structural engineer for any pre-1920 home.
- **Water intrusion / hills:** hillside lots, retaining walls, and drainage (French drains) are recurring inspection issues; look at the property during or after rain. [P] ([JMC Inspections](https://jmcinspections.com/drainage-101/)) Fog-belt (Sunset/Richmond) stucco and window leaks are commonly mentioned. [P/folklore]
- **Pest report Section 1 vs 2:** Section 1 = active infestation/damage (fungus/dry rot, termites); Section 2 = conditions likely to lead to it (earth-to-wood contact, moisture). Statewide convention is seller pays Section 1 and buyer takes Section 2, but it's negotiable. [P] ([Cooper](https://cooperfamilyrealestate.com/blog/california-section-1-section-2-pest-report-explained)) **In SF**, sellers typically disclose the report and sell as-is, with the Section 1 cost priced in rather than repaired or credited. [P/folklore] Lenders rarely require pest clearance on conventional loans; FHA/VA may. [P/?]
- **RECO + water conservation at sale (DBI, Housing Code ch. 12A):** SF requires a time-of-sale inspection by a DBI-certified RECO/water inspector and a recorded certificate of compliance **before transfer of title**. [D] ([SF.gov water conservation](https://www.sf.gov/comply-water-conservation-requirements), [SF.gov RECO doc](https://www.sf.gov/documents/9911/Certified_Residential_Energy_Conservation_Ordinance_RECO_and_Water_Conservatio_a2YqCUG.pdf))
  - Energy (RECO): attic insulation to R-19 where existing < R-11, weatherstripping doors to unheated areas, water-heater insulation, duct insulation, caulking/sealing. [D] ([SF Environment](https://www.sfenvironment.org/residential-energy-conservation-ordinance), [SF Building Code §1212](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/f34d8cdf-0811-4e2d-8768-67a15f4b6738/sf_building/0-0-0-978))
  - Water: 1.8 gpm showerheads/faucets and 1.28 gpf toilets, and all leaks repaired. **Stricter standards took effect May 15, 2026** (Bldg Code §12A.10), and DBI then granted a temporary exemption letting 1.6 gpf toilets remain "until further notice." [D/P] ([SF.gov](https://www.sf.gov/comply-water-conservation-requirements), [The Front Steps](https://thefrontsteps.com/2026/05/12/water-conservation-update/))
  - Usually handled by the seller; practitioner reports say the obligation can sometimes be shifted to the buyer by agreement. [P/?]
- **"Residential energy performance" disclosure:** I found **no SF time-of-sale energy-performance/benchmark requirement for 1–4 unit homes** beyond RECO. SF's Existing Buildings Ordinance benchmarking targets larger buildings. [?] Verify before stating either way.
- **Smoke/CO detectors, water-heater strapping:** state point-of-sale items (above). SF's RECO/HIS inspection often checks these in practice. [P/?]

## 7. Financing

- **2026 conforming limits, SF County (1 unit):** baseline $832,750; high-balance/"super-conforming" up to **$1,249,125**. Above that is **jumbo**. [D-ish; mirrors FHFA] ([JVM Lending](https://www.jvmlending.com/blog/california-conforming-loan-limits/), [Community First](https://communityfirstmortgage.com/san-francisco-county-conforming-loan-limits-2026/)) High-balance loans price slightly higher than baseline conforming. [P] With median SFH ~$1.86M (Aug 2026), most SFH purchases need a jumbo loan or a very large down payment. [P]
- **Jumbo pricing:** the SF jumbo/conforming spread is reported as narrow (0.10–0.15%) because banks compete for wealthy borrowers; early-2026 30-yr jumbo ~6.25–6.875%. [P] ([JVM Lending](https://www.jvmlending.com/blog/jumbo-loans-in-san-francisco/), [DiVita](https://mycahomeloan.com/bay-area-jumbo-mortgage-rates-2026/)) Portfolio lenders (private banks, credit unions) commonly offer relationship pricing and ARMs. [P/?] Jumbo underwriting: more reserves, often 10–20% down minimum, a second appraisal on large loans. [P/?]
- **Rate buydowns:** permanent (points) or temporary (2-1). Seller-paid buydowns and credits are a slow-market tool; in SF's 2026 bidding-war SFH market, seller credits are rare, while softer condo segments may still negotiate them. [P, inference; no SF data found] [?]
- **Appraisal gaps:** at 124% of list, appraisals often lag contract price on hot SFH deals. Non-contingent financed buyers must cover the gap in cash. [P, inference from the market stats]
- **First-time buyer programs:**
  - **DALP (MOHCD Downpayment Assistance Loan Program):** up to **$500,000** deferred ("silent second") loan, repaid with principal plus a share of appreciation on sale or transfer. Household income ≤200% AMI (limits $218,200 for 1 person up to $436,400 for 9). Can't have owned residential property in the last 3 years. Homebuyer education required. Market-rate homes only. Annual application window, then lottery (2025: closed June 2, lottery June 25). [D] ([SF.gov DALP](https://www.sf.gov/apply-downpayment-loan-buy-market-rate-home), [DALP lottery results](https://www.sf.gov/dalp-lottery-results)) Older or third-party sources cite $375k / 175% AMI — **superseded**. Special tracks exist (e.g., educators). [P] ([Mosaik](https://mosaikrealestate.com/blog/how-to-apply-for-educators-dalp)) 2026-27 closing fee $682. [P/?]
  - **BMR ownership (MOHCD):** units sold, and resold, at restricted prices to income-qualified buyers chosen by lottery via **DAHLIA**; restrictions last the life of the property; resale price set by MOHCD; broker compensation capped (up to 2.5% of the max baseline resale price per side, only if it fits within the max price); upgrades/extra parking can't be added to the resale price. [D] ([SF.gov BMR resale](https://www.sf.gov/resell-your-below-market-rate-bmr-home), [SF.gov BMR programs](https://www.sf.gov/reports--august-2024--below-market-rate-bmr-ownership-programs))
  - Other: CalHFA programs (e.g., Dream For All shared-appreciation, when funded). [P] Not researched in depth here.

## Common misconceptions
1. "Prop I 2024 raised the $10M+ rates" → it was **Prop I 2020** (5.5% / 6%). A *different* Prop I is on the Nov 2026 ballot and redirects revenue without changing rates.
2. "SF requires a sewer lateral inspection at sale" → SF has **none** at point of sale (per practitioner source; confirm with SFPUC).
3. "Escalation clauses are rare in SF" → no data; "rare" is folklore consistent with the sealed offer-date format.
4. "The deposit is 3%" → 3% is the common SF number and the statutory liquidated-damages cap for owner-occupied 1–4 units; 1–3% is the practitioner range.
5. "The loan contingency is 21 days" → the RPA default 17 vs 21 days is **disputed across sources**; check the current form.
