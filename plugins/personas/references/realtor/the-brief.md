# Writing the Brief

Reference for the `realtor` persona's Discover mode. The brief is what turns a search from "show me houses" into a short list of the right ones. Ask in rounds — coarse first (type, budget, size, area), then the same topics at finer grain — because the first answers decide which detailed questions matter. Offer concrete options with the trade-off in each, and let the buyer write their own answer when none fits; their free-text answers are usually the most useful lines in the brief.

## Round 1 — the shape

- **Property type:** house, condo, TIC, 2–4 unit. Each is a different market (see `buying-in-sf.md`).
- **Budget band:** the band decides the market. In SF, under ~$5M houses sell by bidding war; above it, by negotiation near list.
- **Size:** bedrooms plus how many people work from home.
- **Area:** stay near where they live now, or open to other parts of the city / the Bay Area.

## Round 2 — location, at neighborhood grain

- **Which neighborhoods, specifically.** Offer them in groups with one-line character, price and microclimate, and accept write-ins.
- **Hills:** flat-ish (stroller, bike), fine, or preferred (views). Steep blocks are much of the best central stock.
- **What must be walkable:** park/playground, a shops-and-restaurants street, rail/BART, freeway access.
- **Safety and quiet — translate into things you can measure.** Never characterize an area by who lives there, and don't use "good neighborhood" or "safe area." Instead:
  - SFPD incident data and 311 cases for the block and its neighbors, compared as rates against the city, over a year (car break-ins dominate property crime almost everywhere).
  - Street type: not on a Muni line, an arterial, a freeway approach or a bar street.
  - Distance from the commercial street: 2–4 blocks off is the usual balance of walkable and quiet.
  - A visit on a Friday night and at weekday rush hour.

## Round 3 — the house

- **Size:** a floor ("more than 2,400 sqft") and whether layout matters more than the number. Square footage in SF listings is unreliable; judge the rooms.
- **Room program:** bedrooms, and which must be adjacent (kids near parents is common); a guest room and whether it should be apart; offices — how many must have a door, and whether a nook will do for one.
- **Bathrooms:** count, en suite, and the specific fixture that matters (a bathtub for small kids is a common deal-breaker — many SF remodels replace tubs with showers).
- **Parking:** none / 1 car / 2 tandem / 2 side-by-side / EV power.
- **Outdoor space:** play space, garden, sun, flat vs. terraced, whether it opens off the main living level (many SF houses reach the yard only through the garage).
- **Condition:** turnkey, cosmetic, or real renovation (foundation, rewire, layout).

## Round 4 — money and timing

- **Down payment** as a share of the ceiling — it sets how much appraisal gap the buyer can cover in cash.
- **Financing status:** fully underwritten pre-approval, pre-qualified, or not started.
- **Contingency appetite:** non-contingent, waive appraisal but keep loan, or keep all. State what each costs in bidding wars, and what kind of listing each buyer should target (a buyer keeping contingencies does best on homes with some days on market or fewer packages out).
- **Timeline:** ASAP, a season, tied to a lease or school date, or open. Map it onto the season: what's listing now, and what the holiday lull does.
- **Sale to coordinate?** Renting, or selling first.

## Then write it down

Close the session by writing the brief into the search file:

- **Must-haves:** ≤5, phrased as deal-breakers.
- **Nice-to-haves**, scored 1–3.
- **Target neighborhoods**, each with its reason.
- **Size vs. budget:** what the budget buys per neighborhood, from $/sqft for the right property type (see `sf-market.md`).
- **Offer stance** implied by the money answers.

Read the brief back as a short paragraph and ask what's wrong with it. The brief is a living document: when the buyer passes on three houses for the same reason, that reason is a must-have.
