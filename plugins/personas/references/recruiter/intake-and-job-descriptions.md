# Intake and Job Descriptions

Reference for the `recruiter` persona: turning a hiring manager's request into a defined role, writing the job description, and getting the pay range and posting legally right. As of October 2026. Every claim is tagged:

- **[D]** documented — statute, government agency, peer-reviewed study, meta-analysis
- **[P]** practitioner — recruiting blogs, vendor content, platform data; real but self-interested or anecdotal
- **[?]** conflicting or unverified — sources disagree, or only a weak source; verify before stating as fact

Pay-transparency law moves every legislative session. Treat every legal line as a dated snapshot and check the statute or agency page before advising on a live posting. This is not legal advice; route edge cases (multi-state remote, commission roles, union roles) to employment counsel.

## 1. The intake meeting

The intake is where most hiring failures are decided: a req opened on a vague brief produces a loop that can't agree what "good" is. Lou Adler's "performance profile" framing — define the 6–8 things the person must *accomplish*, not the skills they must *have* — is the practitioner backbone here. [P] ([Lou Adler Group](https://www.louadlergroup.com/elevating-job-descriptions-a-performance-based-approach/))

### Questions that define the role
- **The problem.** What is not getting done today because this person isn't here? What breaks, slows, or stays unbuilt? If the manager can't name it, the req isn't ready.
- **Why now.** What changed — new product line, attrition, a scaling wall, a backfill? A backfill invites "clone the last person"; a new mandate invites a unicorn list. Both need challenging.
- **What's been tried.** Did someone already own this? Contractors, a reorg, an internal transfer that didn't take? This surfaces whether the gap is really headcount or something else (scope, prioritization, tooling).
- **Success at 30 / 90 / 365 days.** Concrete outcomes, not traits: "by day 90 has shipped X and owns the on-call rotation for Y." These become the JD's "in your first six months you will…" lines *and* the interview rubric — write them once, reuse them.
- **Must-have vs. trainable vs. nice-to-have.** For each requirement ask: could a strong person learn this in the first 90 days? If yes, it's trainable, not a must-have. Aim for 3–5 must-haves; everything else is a bonus or gets cut.
- **The level, and why.** Tie level to *scope of impact* in the company's ladder (owns a feature / a system / a cross-team direction), not to years. Ask: which existing person on the team is this most like, and is it above or below them? The answer anchors both the band and internal equity.
- **Who they'll work with.** Manager, peers, cross-functional partners, direct reports if any. Determines who sits on the loop and which collaboration signals matter.
- **Constraints.** Location/remote policy, on-call, travel, visa sponsorship, start date, budget (band and whether it can flex). Get the budget *now* — a band discovered at offer stage kills deals.

### How to challenge the hiring manager
- **Credential proxies.** "5+ years" and "CS degree" are cheap filters, not predictors. A meta-analysis of 81 samples found pre-hire work experience correlates ~.06 with job performance and .00 with turnover (corrected); even *relevant* experience only reached ~.07. [D] ([Van Iddekinge et al. 2019, *Personnel Psychology*](https://digitalcommons.unf.edu/unf_faculty_publications/785/)) Ask: "What would someone with five years know that someone with three exceptional years wouldn't? Can we test that directly?" Then write the thing itself as the requirement.
- **Degree requirements.** Dropping them from the posting is not the same as changing who gets hired: a Burning Glass Institute / HBS study found fewer than 1 in 700 hires in 2023 benefited from degree-requirement removal, and ~45% of firms that removed them made no meaningful change in non-degree hiring. [D/P, institute report] ([HBS summary](https://www.hbs.edu/bigs/joseph-fuller-college-degree-gap), [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/employers-failing-at-skills-based-hiring/707897/)) If the manager agrees to drop it, also make sure screeners stop filtering on it.
- **Pedigree** ("ex-FAANG", "top school"). Same problem as degrees: a proxy for skill that also narrows the pool along class and demographic lines. Ask what the pedigree is standing in for, and assess that.
- **Rockstar wish lists.** A list of 15 "requirements" usually means the manager hasn't decided what matters. Force-rank it; the bottom two-thirds become nice-to-haves or vanish.
- **Cloning the last person.** "Find me another Priya" encodes one person's history, including the parts that were luck. Re-derive the role from the *current* problem; the team may now need a different shape.
- **Unicorn combos.** Staff-level distributed-systems depth + ML + frontend + people management at a senior IC band doesn't exist in volume. Show the manager the market data (section 5) for the combination and make them choose: narrow the scope, raise the band, or accept a longer search.
- **Unassessable criteria.** "Culture fit", "passion", "rockstar energy", "thrives in ambiguity." If no interview step can produce evidence for it, it's either cut or rewritten into an observable behavior ("has driven a project to completion without a defined spec — tell us about one"). Structured interviews against defined criteria are the top-ranked selection method in the most recent large meta-analytic update, which is the payoff for doing this work at intake. [D] ([Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens 2022, *J. Applied Psychology*](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/6894/))

### Intake output (one page, agreed in writing)
Problem statement · why now · 30/90/365 outcomes · 3–5 must-haves · trainable list · level + rationale · band (min–max) · location/remote · loop panel and what each interviewer assesses · sourcing targets · timeline · who approves the offer.

## 2. Writing the job description

### Outcome-based vs. requirements list
- Lead with the work: what the team does, what this person will own, and "in your first six months you will…" bullets taken straight from the intake's 30/90/365 outcomes. [P] ([Lou Adler](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/dump-your-job-descriptions-hire-stronger-talent-lou-adler))
- Keep requirements short and genuinely required. Put the rest under "nice to have" or cut it. A long list does more to deter qualified people than to filter unqualified ones (see myth below), and every unassessable line is a promise the loop can't keep.

### Myth: "Women apply only when 100% qualified; men at 60%"
- **Origin.** Not a study. The line comes from an anecdote by a Hewlett-Packard executive quoted in a McKinsey Quarterly piece ("A business case for women", Desvaux, Devillard-Hoellinger & Meaney). When Curt Rice asked the McKinsey authors, they said it came from confidential executive interviews — there is no HP dataset to examine. It was also originally about *internal promotions*, not job ads. [D/P] ([Curt Rice, 2014](https://www.huffingtonpost.co.uk/curt-rice/how-mckinseys-story-became-sheryl-sandbergs-statistic-and-why-it-didnt-deserve-to_b_5198744.html)) It spread via *Lean In*, *The Confidence Code*, and Tara Mohr's 2014 HBR piece, which repeated it as "a Hewlett Packard internal report." [P] ([Mohr, HBR 2014](https://hbr.org/2014/08/why-women-dont-apply-for-jobs-unless-theyre-100-qualified))
- **Mohr's own survey** (≈1,000 mostly US professionals, self-selected) is more useful than the stat she quoted: the top reason for not applying was the same for both sexes — "I didn't think they would hire me since I didn't meet the qualifications" (41% women, 46% men). The gendered gap was in *following the stated rules*: 15% of women vs. 8% of men didn't apply because they were "following the guidelines about who should apply." Her read: the barrier is a misperception about how hiring works, not confidence. [P, non-random survey] ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2014/08/why-women-dont-apply-for-jobs-unless-theyre-100-qualified))
- **Experimental tests.** A Behavioural Insights Team online experiment (10,000+ job seekers) found men willing to apply at ~52% fit and women at ~56% — a small gap, concentrated among less-qualified applicants; when requirements were assessed one by one, women's self-ratings were not lower. [P, large experiment, not peer-reviewed] ([BIT, 2022](https://www.bi.team/blogs/women-only-apply-when-100-qualified-fact-or-fake-news/)) A peer-reviewed test found **no** gender difference in application intention at either 60% or 100% fit, though women reported a stronger desire to feel prepared. [D] ([Salwender & Stahlberg 2024, *Eur. J. Social Psychology*](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/67751/1/Euro%20J%20Social%20Psych%20-%202024%20-%20Salwender%20-%20Do%20women%20only%20apply%20when%20they%20are%20100%20qualified%20whereas%20men%20already%20apply%20when-1.pdf))
- **Platform data.** LinkedIn reports women apply to ~20% fewer jobs than men but are ~16% more likely to be hired once they apply. [P, platform data; the same report re-cites the HP myth] ([LinkedIn Gender Insights](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/how-women-find-jobs-gender-report))
- **What to do with it.** Don't cite 60/100. The defensible advice is the same anyway: list only true must-haves, label the rest optional, and say explicitly "if you meet most of these, apply" — the evidence suggests people (women somewhat more) treat stated requirements as rules.

### Gendered / coded language
- **The foundational study.** Gaucher, Friesen & Kay (2011, *JPSP*, 5 studies): ads in male-dominated fields used more masculine-coded words ("competitive", "dominant", "leader"); masculine-worded ads were rated less appealing by women, mediated by lower anticipated *belonging* — not by women judging themselves less skilled. Men were largely unaffected. [D] ([paper PDF](https://irsj.ca/assets/papers/2011_gaucher_friesen_and_kay_evidence_that_gendered_wording_in_job_advertisements_exists_and_sustains_gender_inequality.pdf))
- **Replications and field tests disagree on size.** [?] overall:
  - Replicated for start-ups but **not** for established firms. [D] ([Seong & Parker 2024, *Strategic Entrepreneurship J.*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/sej.1489))
  - ~300k postings, 485k job seekers on a real platform: gendered wording had "no effects that matter in practice" on who applied. [D] ([Castilla & Rho 2023, *Management Science*](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/centers-initiatives/institute-work-and-employment-research/gendered-language-job-postings-has-little-effect-applicant-behavior-new-research-finds))
  - Randomized field experiment (Latin American tech job board): neutral wording raised women's applicant share in design/marketing roles (~12.6 pp) but not in programming, and the effect faded as more ads were treated. [D, NBER working paper] ([Del Carpio & Fujiwara, NBER w31314](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31314))
  - Four field and lab studies (N ≈ 38k): swapping masculine words for neutral synonyms increased applications from women and from men less identified with masculinity. [D] ([He & Kang 2025, *PNAS*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39928868))
- **Practical stance.** Neutral wording is cheap and low-risk, so do it — but expect a modest effect, and don't sell it to a manager as the fix for a homogeneous pipeline. Sourcing and requirement inflation matter more.
- **Tools.** Textio (commercial) and free "gender decoder" tools built on the Gaucher word lists flag coded terms. Evidence for vendor outcome claims (faster fills, more diverse pools) is the vendor's own customer data, not independent trials. [P] Use them as a lint pass, not a guarantee.
- "Rockstar", "ninja", "crush it", "work hard, play hard", "young and hungry" (also an age-discrimination flag under the ADEA): cut. [P]

### Length, structure, what to cut
- LinkedIn's analysis of 4.5M postings: posts under 300 words got 8.4% more applications per view than average; 301–600 words did 3.4% worse. [P, platform data] ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/talent-acquisition/stats-that-will-change-the-way-you-write-job-posts)) More *applications* isn't automatically better; the aim is more *qualified* ones. Short and specific beats long and generic.
- Skeleton: role title (in the company's leveling language) · 2–3 sentence team/mission pitch · what you'll do (outcome bullets) · what we're looking for (3–5 must-haves) · nice to have · pay range and benefits summary · location/remote · how the interview process works · EEO statement.
- Cut: company boilerplate longer than a paragraph, duplicated "responsibilities" and "requirements", tech-stack laundry lists, adjectives without evidence ("fast-paced", "world-class"), and anything the loop won't assess.
- Title: use a market-legible title (e.g. "Senior Software Engineer, Payments") rather than internal jargon or inflated titles that don't match the band; it drives search visibility. [P]

### Include the salary range
- Required in a growing list of jurisdictions (section 3), and effectively required for any US-remote posting.
- Evidence it changes the market: after Colorado's 2021 law, postings with pay info rose from ~35% to ~70%, and posted salaries rose ~3.6% (wages 1.3–3.6% across datasets), including for incumbents. [D, economics working paper] ([Arnold, Quach & Taska](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4186234))
- Market norm: 50.4% of US Indeed postings had explicit pay in July 2026, the series high. [P, platform data] ([Indeed Hiring Lab tracker](https://github.com/hiring-lab/pay-transparency))
- Post a range you'd actually pay a new hire. Absurdly wide ranges ("$90k–$300k") invite enforcement and signal the company hasn't leveled the role.

### Salary-history bans
- Roughly 18 states plus DC restrict asking about or relying on pay history for private employers (including CA, CO, CT, DE, HI, IL, ME, MD, MA, NV, NJ, NY, OR, RI, VT, VA, WA, DC), plus cities such as NYC, Philadelphia and Cleveland. [P, tracker; exact count varies by source — [?]] ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/talent-acquisition/state-state-salary-history-bans-pay-transparency-laws))
- **Default for a US company hiring anywhere: never ask.** Ask instead for expectations ("what are you targeting?") and anchor offers on the band and level. Recruiters and agencies acting for the employer are covered too.

### EU Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970)
- Transposition deadline **7 June 2026**. As of late July 2026 only Italy, Lithuania, Malta and Slovakia had fully transposed, Greece shortly after; most member states missed it and several (incl. Germany, Spain, Sweden) had no draft. [P] ([L&E Global, Jul 2026](https://leglobal.law/2026/07/28/eu-pay-transparency-member-states-near-transposition-finish-line/), [DCI](https://blog.dciconsult.com/the-transposition-deadline-has-passed)) Obligations bind employers only via national law, so check the specific country.
- **Art. 5(1):** applicants are entitled to the initial pay or its range, based on objective gender-neutral criteria — in the vacancy notice, before the interview, or otherwise early enough for informed negotiation. The Directive does not strictly require the range *in the ad*; some national laws will. [D] ([Directive text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32023L0970))
- **Art. 5(2):** employers may not ask applicants about their pay history. **Art. 5(3):** vacancy notices and job titles must be gender-neutral and recruitment non-discriminatory. [D] (same)

## 4. Levels and comp-band inputs for the req

What the recruiter needs before posting: the level (from the company ladder), the band for that level and location tier, the equity guideline, and whether the band can flex. If the company has no ladder, the intake must at least fix scope (team / system / org) and a defensible range.

Data sources — what each *is*; never quote a number from them without pulling it fresh:
- **Levels.fyi** — crowdsourced, self-reported tech comp (base, stock, bonus) by company and level; a growing share is verified via offer letters/pay stubs. Normalizes levels across companies by scope and transfer data, which makes it the best public tool for "what is a Google L5 equivalent here." Skews to large tech and to people motivated to report. [P] ([Levels.fyi data guide](https://www.levels.fyi/reports/archive/guides/Levels.fyi%20Compensation%20Data%20Guide.pdf))
- **Pave** — paid benchmarking built from customer HRIS and cap-table integrations ("give to get"); near-real-time salary and equity by role, level, location, funding stage. Strong for venture-backed tech. [P] ([Pave support](https://support.pave.com/hc/en-us/articles/4430975849495-Market-Data-Connections-Overview))
- **Carta Total Compensation** — salary and equity benchmarks from Carta's private-company cap-table data, updated at least quarterly; now open to non-Carta customers. Best for startup equity norms. [P] ([Carta](https://carta.com/blog/expanding-ctc/))
- **Radford (Aon)**, Mercer, WTW — traditional survey-based benchmarks submitted by participating companies, updated annually/semi-annually; standard at public and late-stage companies. [P] ([overview](https://ravio.com/blog/radford-alternatives-for-compensation-management))
- **Public:** BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (broad occupation codes, lagging, no equity) and the job postings themselves — competitors' ranges in pay-transparency states are now free market data.

Sanity checks: the posted range must contain the internal-equity point (what peers at that level earn), and the top must be reachable — a range whose top no one is ever offered breaches the "good faith" standard in CA, NYC, MA and others.

## 5. Worked example (Example Co)

**The request:** "We need a senior backend engineer ASAP. Rockstar, 7+ years, CS degree from a good school, Go and Kubernetes, ideally ex-big-tech."

**Intake findings:**
- Problem: the payments service has no dedicated owner; two incidents last quarter; new EU payment methods are blocked behind it.
- Why now: the engineer who built it left; roadmap commits EU launch in Q2.
- What's been tried: rotating on-call across the team — knowledge stayed shallow.
- Success: 30 days — on-call shadow, has read every incident postmortem. 90 days — primary on-call, has shipped one reliability fix. 365 days — has led the EU payment-methods integration and written the service's runbook.
- Must-haves (4): has owned a production service with on-call responsibility; has designed for idempotency and failure in a system moving money or similar critical state; writes clear design docs; debugs across service boundaries.
- Trainable: Go (team is fine with strong Java/Rust/Python backgrounds), Kubernetes specifics, payment-provider APIs.
- Cut: "7+ years" (replaced by the ownership must-have), degree, "good school", "ex-big-tech", "rockstar" — none assessable or predictive.
- Level: Senior (L4 on Example Co's ladder) — owns one system end to end; not Staff, because no cross-team technical direction is expected.
- Band: pulled for L4, remote-US tier, from Pave + Levels.fyi peers; internal-equity checked against the two current L4s.

**JD skeleton:**

> **Senior Software Engineer, Payments — Example Co** (Remote, US)
> Example Co's payments service moves every dollar our customers earn. We're looking for the engineer who will own it.
> **In your first six months you will:** take over primary on-call for payments · ship reliability fixes that reduce incident rate · lead the design of support for new EU payment methods · write the runbook the next engineer inherits.
> **You'll bring:** ownership of a production service including on-call · experience designing for failure and idempotency where correctness matters · clear written design docs · debugging across service boundaries.
> **Nice to have:** Go, Kubernetes, payment-provider integrations. We'll help you learn whichever you don't have — if you meet most of the above, apply.
> **Pay:** $[min]–$[max] base + equity + benefits [one-line summary]. Range reflects what we expect to pay a new hire at this level, nationally.
> **Process:** recruiter call → technical conversation with the hiring manager → system-design and debugging interviews → team conversation. About three weeks end to end.
> [EEO statement]

Before posting: run a gender-decoder lint pass, confirm range meets CA/NY/WA/IL/MA/CO/CT/VA requirements (it's remote-US, so all apply), add the Colorado application-close date, and confirm screeners won't filter on degree or years.
